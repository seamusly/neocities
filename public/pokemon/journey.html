Growing Up

Growing Up

So, I'm a 90s kid. I just barely made the cut actually. And so it probably won't surprise most people to hear that I was into Pokémon growing up. One of my earliest memories -- and I really do mean as far back as being a conscious human being goes for me -- is laying on the bed of the attic room my parents and I lived in and watching Pokémon Heroes.

I watched it a lot. Even now, as I was looking up GIFs to embed into this writing, I kind of just had to stop and marvel at the opening sequence. It takes me back in a way I'm not sure many things can.

(This section got a bit long-winded so the rest is underneath a collapsible! You can click the star at the top of this section to expand / collapse the full text!)

I wasn't fortunate enough to afford many video games or consoles in my childhood. The only handheld I actually owned before 2006-ish was the Game Boy SP. I knew people who had games, so I would only get to play whenever I was visiting a friend or whenever my relatives weren't playing themselves and would actually let me use their console. That also meant I could only play the games that they actually owned, and my cousins were more into Playstation back then. So, most of my interaction with the franchise came from the anime.

I got to play Pokémon Blue whenever I visited a friend and he let me have his GBA. I remember starting up a fresh save several times back then, usually only making it as far as Cerulean City and Bill before I had to go home. Don't think anyone I knew had any of the Gen 2 games. He and his brother got a GameCube at some point too but they were more excited about their PS1. We ended up playing Spyro more than anything, but I desperately wanted to ask if we could play Gale of Darkness instead. I just never did.

Fast-forward to Gen 3 and I finally have my own Game Boy SP, but still no Pokémon games. Back then, my family and I would go abroad to visit my mom's side of the family almost yearly in Asia. At some point, my aunts bought me a cart for LeafGreen. It was black and the sticker had a picture of the trainer's overworld sprite over top of a fiery graphic -- and it definitely said "LeafGreen". Most times, it wouldn't save my game and I had to start over from the beginning. I'd try to keep the Game Boy on for as long as I could to try and make it further, knowing my game might not be there the next time I booted it up.

But knock-offs aside, I did eventually get ahold of an authentic cart for Sapphire and eventually Emerald. I finally had my own games and I could actually play through them on my own time. I, much like many other kids at the time, spent many nights staying up past my bedtime to keep trucking through Hoenn. I restarted my saves a lot then too. I guess my attachment to my little digital creatures wasn't terribly strong back then. And yet, despite probably having upwards of at least a hundred hours or more across all my RSE saves, I never actually beat the game. I don't even think I ever saw the legendaries. I don't even think I ever fought Tate and Liza more than a couple of times, and I probably never fought the eighth gym.

Because here's the thing about my history with not only Pokémon, but with video games as a whole growing up: I was a bit dumb.

I didn't have manuals, I didn't know how to use the internet yet, I didn't fully understand what the characters were saying to me or what any of it meant. Thinking about it now, I don't think I realized I had to do the Aqua/Magma hideouts before heading out to sea. So I probably just aimlessly roamed until I got bored and gave up. The next time I'd pick up the game after that, I would start a new save.

Despite all that, I truly did love playing the games. I was devastated when I'd lost my copy of Emerald on one of the plane rides back to Canada. But it wasn't long after that when my parents bought me an NDS and a copy of Pokémon Ranger and Diamond.
